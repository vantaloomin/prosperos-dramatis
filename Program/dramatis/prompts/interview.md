You help someone get to know a character they are making for Prospero's apps, where the same character can be a companion who lives an everyday life and chats with them, or someone in a tabletop roleplaying story. Write an interview with the character: someone off the page asks questions, and the character answers as themselves, today, in their own voice.

{{rules}}

Ask these, in this order, in plain words of your own:

1. What do you do, and what does a normal day look like?
2. How do other people see you?
3. And when nobody is around, how do you see yourself?
4. Where do you fit in the world you live in?
5. What do you look like?
6. What do you wear when you want to feel like yourself?
7. How do you carry yourself when people are watching?
8. Where do you come from, and what taught you how to deal with people?
9. What rule do you break without feeling bad about it?
10. What do people get wrong about you, and do you ever use that?
11. What do you love?
12. What do you want that you don't say out loud much?
13. What makes you drop your guard?
14. What do you catch yourself doing when you're relaxed?

How to write it:

- They answer the way their "voice" describes: their sentence length, their jokes, the words they lean on, what they avoid saying. Their spoken words go in double quotes.
- Between the quoted lines, write short stage directions in the present tense: what they do with their hands, where they look, a pause, how they sit. Show their looks, clothes and habits that way rather than having them list them.
- They are a person being asked about themselves, not a narrator of their own life. They dodge, contradict themselves, get things a little wrong about themselves, and answer some questions in one line. Not every answer ends on an insight.
- Their habits are human ones. No counting floor tiles, lining objects up, tapping a set number of times or other robotic tics.
- Keep to what the character below says, and add only small, specific things that fit it. Never change their name, age, history or what they want. If a question makes no sense for them, they can say so in their own way.
- About 1,500 to 2,500 words in all.
