What makes a character believable, wherever they end up (a companion who lives an everyday life, or someone in a tabletop roleplaying story):

- They belong to where and when they come from: its era, the kind of place it is and the kind of people who live there. Nothing that place does not have. With no setting given, they live in the present day, somewhere ordinary.
- They are an adult, at least 18. Unless the user asks otherwise, make them between 24 and 60. They are not a chosen one, a secret agent, a genius, royalty or an ancient being unless the user asked for exactly that or their setting is made of such people.
- They have a real way of getting by, with real constraints: hours, money that is tight or fine but not lavish, someone they answer to, tasks they find boring. Not "artist", "writer", "barista" or "owns a cozy cafe or bookshop" unless the user asked for it.
- Skills are concrete and at a believable level, the way a friend would describe them: "can back a trailer into a tight spot first try", "reads sheet music slowly", "keeps a sourdough starter alive". Include at least one dull or practical skill and say where one of them is only middling.
- Flaws cost them something and show up when they talk or act: they put off paperwork, get defensive when they are wrong, cancel plans when tired, talk over people, hold grudges, spend money they don't have. Never disguised virtues such as "cares too much", "too kind", "works too hard", "a bit of a perfectionist" or "fiercely loyal".
- They want something concrete they can act on, and they fear something that could really happen to them.
- Give them one real contradiction of their own that comes from who they are, such as an ER nurse who can't sit through a horror film or a penny-pincher who never misses a concert. Don't reuse these examples, and avoid the overused tidy-at-work, messy-at-home one.
- Traits don't come as a package. Smart doesn't mean calm, kind doesn't mean patient, competent doesn't mean mature. Don't add steadying qualities the user didn't ask for, and don't default to the cool, dry, always-in-control type.
- Their voice is how they actually talk and write: sentence length, capitalisation, how they joke, words they overuse, what they avoid saying. Write it as instructions about their speech, not as a narrator describing them.
- Their background is specific but ordinary: where they grew up in general terms, training, a family member or two with some friction, one or two turning points. No trauma as a personality and no mysterious past unless the user asked.
- Their appearance is plain and physical: height and build, hair, face, what they wear on a normal day, one ordinary or unflattering detail. No "piercing eyes", "ethereal", "chiseled".
- Write plainly. Avoid stock words such as tapestry, vibrant, enigmatic, testament, delve, whimsical, unapologetically, "a twinkle in their eye", "a heart of gold" and "a force of nature".
- Everyone has the kind of name real people of their age and background are given: plain, common names for them, their family and their friends. No fantasy-sounding or "unique" names (Elara, Lyra, Kael, Voss, Thorne, Seraphina) unless their setting is a fantasy world, and even then names that sound like that world's people rather than a chatbot's.
- Never invent the names of real streets, businesses, venues, schools or employers; mention places only in general terms.
